# Accounts Flow

A dependency-free static application showing the attached PersonAI account history as monochrome particle flows. The English interface uses the requested name, Camille; source metadata retains Sarah as the recorded synthetic persona.

## Data

Money is stored in integer euro cents. `model.js` validates all 1,139 running balances and pairs all 92 internal transfers. It groups transactions by their exact recorded date into 731 consecutive days (9 October 2018 to 8 October 2020), including 186 days without operations and 29 February 2020. Income and spending exclude internal transfers. Daily averages include all observed days.

The history reconciles to €5,800.00 opening cash + €91,946.28 external income − €77,506.76 external spending = €20,239.52 closing cash.

## Importing a history

The **Import JSON** button accepts the original `personia.account-history` version 1 format: `included.accounts`, `data` operations, and `meta.startDate` / `meta.endDate`. Each account supplies its ID, key, name, opening and closing balances; operations supply their account, date, amount and before/after balances. Integer cent fields are preferred, with euro fields supported. Internal transfers retain `parsedData.transferId` and their counterparty account references, so all related accounts must be included in the export. Account names, holder, banks, income labels and expense categories come from the uploaded file.

Files are read and validated entirely in browser memory, with no upload, network request or local storage. Reloading returns to the demo; **Use demo** also restores it immediately. The importer reconciles every running balance, final account balance and transfer pair before replacing the view. Invalid exports leave the current dataset intact and display an accessible error. Successful imports reset and pause the animation at their initial balances while retaining the playback speed and loop preference. All date, overview, detail and node controls refresh, and the diagram adapts to different node counts.

This visualization supports EUR histories, including negative initial, intermediate and final balances. Overdrawn funds appear as red particles in accounts, on their outgoing paths and in expense categories. Red expense particles retain their funding colour after later income repays the overdraft, until the monthly expense reset. Cash and liabilities are reconciled separately, so borrowing and repayment preserve the signed account balance. Fractional particles are recombined only with others of the same colour. It imposes no application cap on years, accounts, income sources, operations, file size or particle count. Calendar dates and ledger reconciliation are still validated. Uploaded text is rendered as text or escaped before insertion into HTML.

The full period determines the date controls, playback endpoint, history duration and daily averages. The timeline summarises every day into the available display pixels without reducing the selectable date range. Each diagram column grows independently from its own node count. Extended diagrams scroll vertically while the backing canvas stays viewport-sized; all nodes remain available through the accessible list and details. Empty days share unchanged ledger snapshots, motion compilation skips inactive days, and the inspection frame array uses lazy indexed access rather than allocating a property for every calendar day. Ledger validation runs without constructing the complete daily model during parsing.

## Animation

One full particle represents €10; a smaller particle represents a residual amount. Circle area follows amount on a common scale. Income clouds show that day's income. Expense clouds and amounts accumulate through the calendar month, growing with actual particle arrivals. They persist between days (including days without activity), and reset only at the next month. Categories remain grey until their first arrival in that month. Account balances and retained particles carry forward continuously across every day and month boundary. The first frame includes the recorded initial balances: €600 current account, €2,500 Livret A, €700 project savings and €2,000 life insurance.

Income, transfers and expenses share the same departure window. They move concurrently rather than in stages by type. Opening cash is used first, so existing funds can leave while income arrives. When an outgoing payment needs incoming money, only that money's next hop waits for its arrival. Recorded order is used for allocating funds; the source does not provide intraday timestamps. Departures remain bell-shaped, with fast leaders and slower tails. Spending particles fade only on the last calendar day of each month. The final partial month remains visible at the end of the history. Trips can now cross several days; their paths and account cash remain continuous at date boundaries.

`simulation.js` retains the exact daily ledger for financial checks. `motion.js` compiles one continuous set of 10,727 token histories across the complete timeline for rendering. Splits, subsequent transfers and fractional recombination wait for preceding arrivals, preserving every cent while several days of trips overlap. Seeking reconstructs this scene deterministically without erasing in-flight cash.

Playback defaults to 1× (0.75 seconds per day), retaining the previous 4× timing. The available multipliers are 1×, 2×, 4×, 8× and 16×; the former slower settings are removed. Direct particle trips take four times their previous elapsed duration at that default (the movement spans 16× more calendar time); organic drift also runs at one quarter of its previous speed. The speed selector scales playback from this setup. Pause, speed, looping, date selection, daily timeline scrubbing and keyboard day navigation remain available. The calendar stops at the final day while any final trips settle, then playback stops or loops; seeking to the endpoint shows the fully settled balances. Pause freezes both time and organic motion. Hiding the tab pauses playback. Hover reveals circle outlines and connectors; Account and income details show the chosen day's operations; expense details show the selected month's transactions through the chosen day, matching the cumulative amount. Account details distinguish the recorded initial balance from the source start-of-day balance. The displayed rolling balance follows the continuous particle ledger, including slow movements in progress. Accounts show cents, and any positive sub-cent interpolation is shown as “< €0.01” rather than rounded to zero. The source current account starts at €600 and has 36 genuine zero closing balances across the history. Controls remain fixed at the bottom with measured page clearance. Play/Pause sits to the left of the timeline; the restart button is removed, with the date and playback settings above. The introductory import copy, portrait/date heading and playback footer captions are removed. Overview labels, values and units use half their previous font sizes at every responsive breakpoint, with proportionate spacing. Overview numbers are bold, while labels, units and the average separator retain regular weight. Import feedback remains visible only when there is a status or error to display.

## Verification

Run `node --test tests/*.test.mjs`. Financial tests use Node; UI tests use the real application with a lightweight DOM harness and the real `@napi-rs/canvas` renderer from `CODEX_PRIMARY_RUNTIME_NODE_MODULES`. Tests cover all daily ledgers, empty days, leap day, exact totals, concurrent three-way flows, money conservation at sampled times and split events, initial balances, carried account particles across all month boundaries, monthly spending retention/reset, expense pagination, continuous multi-day motion conservation, renamed 1× baseline timing / 0.25× particle drift and 2× through 16× playback, precise current-account display, overdraft creation, partial and full repayment, negative-account transfers, colour persistence, repeated zero crossings, cached seeking, date controls, playback/pause/replay/loop/speed, daily details, hover and canvas rendering at narrow and wide sizes.

Native browser QA is unavailable in this managed execution environment. DOM/canvas tests do not claim browser-engine coverage. No dependency installation or build is required for deployment.

Import tests use a small synthetic fixture in the original export schema and cover reconciliation, cent/euro values, transfer references, reverse transaction order, invalid exports, ID collisions, non-Latin labels, zero activity, file selection, failed-import retention, adaptive layout, escaped text and restoring the demo during a pending file read. Extended checks cover 131 years, 120 accounts, multiple income sources, more than 50,000 operations, an opening balance above the old particle threshold, the calendar endpoint in year 9999, complete timeline-bin totals, scrolled node selection and exact final balances. The original 1,139-operation export is additionally checked locally without committing or serving the source file.
